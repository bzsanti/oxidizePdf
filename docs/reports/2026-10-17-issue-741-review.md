Initial unmodified product: six expected failures and one serialization control PASS. The QR found two further boundary defects: cross-subject combination with xml:base and lack of a pre-recursion depth guard. Each has a separate failing public regression, followed by passing final contracts. No stack overflow was executed or claimed: source inspection established recursive tokenizer descent, and the 129-level input demonstrated absence of the required bounded behavior.

Normative reference: [W3C RDF/XML property attributes and namespace identity](https://www.w3.org/TR/rdf-syntax-grammar/#section-Syntax-property-attributes). Dependency behavior inspected in the downloaded source and [roxmltree ParsingOptions](https://docs.rs/roxmltree/0.21.1/roxmltree/struct.ParsingOptions.html). This is the supported XMP identifier profile, not a full RDF graph engine. XML fragments with undeclared prefixes are malformed; the earlier tests were repaired to wrap snippets in valid namespace-qualified RDF instead of preserving that accidental acceptance.

## Hallazgos

1. **Resolved: relative RDF subjects with xml:base could be combined incorrectly.** The initial subject comparison used raw rdf:about strings. Different bases made identical raw strings refer to distinct documents. Final require_document_subject rejects unsupported xml:base resolution before recording identifier values. RED/GREEN: review-red.log and final-contracts.log.
2. **Resolved: tree-parser recursion was not bounded by the node limit.** roxmltree tokenizer.rs recursively calls parse_element/parse_content. Final xmp_packet::parse performs an iterative quick-xml depth check before tree allocation/recursion. It checks empty tags too and rejects DTDs. RED/GREEN: depth-red.log and final-contracts.log; depth 128 accepted, 129 rejected.

No unresolved findings within #741. Ordinary-property aliases/entities, omitted keywords and serializer escaping are known #763 requirements, already tracked OPEN and scheduled next. They do not prevent the identifier parser contract from being demonstrated; they do prevent treating the whole XMP round-trip repair as complete.
